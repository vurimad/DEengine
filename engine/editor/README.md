# editor

`deengine --editor` (dev builds): the engine as it runs, with Dear ImGui panels docked around the game's view (docs/design.md, Editor & tooling).

- **Look** (`theme.h`, `widgets.h`): the user's own editor design, ported: colour roles and sizes at 100%, scaled by the user's interface scale times the display's times 1.2; Source Sans 3 (regular and semibold, OFL, `fonts/`); one look for tooltips, menus, combos, search fields, category headers and property rows (label | value | reset). The panels draw this engine's own data (flecs reflection, the VFS).
- **Layout.** The hierarchy (the cell map in a tab beside it) over the properties on the left; the Playground (the game's view) the whole right side. Along the bottom, as Unreal's: **Resources** (Ctrl+Space, Unreal's content drawer) and **Output**, drawers that come up over the panels when they're wanted (in front of the panels, behind their own menus and lists) (dragged by their top edge to be taller or shorter, their height kept) and go again at a click elsewhere; and **Cmd**, a command line for the console, its suggestions and history the console window's. Panels can be moved and docked anywhere; the layout is kept in `user:/editor/layout.ini` (Window > Reset layout brings back the default). A click on the editor while another window is in front of it is a click at once, not only a way back to it.
- **The Playground** is the scene drawn into a texture at the panel's size (`Engine::viewport`, app/framework.h), shown with `ImGui::Image`, under a toolbar: undo and redo; the tools (select, move, rotate, scale: Q W E R); world or the selection's own axes (a globe or a cube; Ctrl+` switches, as Unreal's; scaling always takes the selection's own); the handles at the selection's pivot or at the centre of its geometry (turning and scaling then go about that centre); snapping to a grid, to angles and to scale steps, each with its step; the camera's speed, the view, framing the selection. The camera moves as in Unity's scene view (`navigation.h`): the right button held over the scene flies it (WASD, the mouse to look, the wheel its speed; the keys are the tools' otherwise), the wheel zooms toward a pivot kept ahead of the camera, a middle drag pans, Alt and a left drag orbits about the pivot, Alt and a right drag zooms, F eases the camera back to frame the selection and makes it the pivot. A left click (never the right) selects the mesh under the mouse, exactly: in the editor the G-buffer writes each pixel's instance into one more target, and a click copies back the one under it (`Engine::editor_overlay`, `SceneRenderer::set_editor_overlay`); the answer comes a few frames later. The ground and the sky select nothing. The selection (and what's attached to it) is outlined in orange around its real shape, solid where it's seen and fainter where something stands in front of it: its meshes are drawn again alone into a mask, without TAA's jitter so the line keeps still, and an anti-aliased band is drawn around the mask over the finished image (`shaders/scene/outline.slang`). The selection's handles (`gizmo.h`) work as Unreal Engine 4's, in the axis colours, yellow under the mouse, the same size on screen at any distance, every part edged dark so it reads on bright ground and sky as on dark. Moving: a thick arrow along each axis with a shaded cone, a corner between each two axes to move in their plane, a sphere in the middle to move in the screen's plane. While a move is dragged only what it goes along shows (its axis, or its plane and that plane's two axes), with a ring where it began, a dashed line from there, and how far in centimetres beside the line. Turning: a ring round each axis, a sphere just inside them, a white dot at the centre. The half of each ring facing the camera is a band with a dark rim, as wide on screen however the ring is turned and tapering where it turns away; the far halves are thin, faint lines and can't be grabbed through the sphere; the ring seen most face on lies under the others. Dragging along a band turns about its axis; the handles stay as they are, and the turn shows on the dragged ring in the band's style: the wedge swept filled in, a bright band along the ring over it, a ruler round the ring from the grab (a tick each 5 degrees, longer each 15, longest each 45 with its angle; those passed in yellow, those round the far side faint), arrows for the ways to drag, and the angle in degrees inside the wedge. Dragging inside the sphere, off the rings, rolls the selection any way, as a ball under the mouse (a radian for each radius dragged), the angle over the sphere. Scaling: the moving handles' layout with a cube where each cone is, a triangle across each corner to scale two axes, a cube in the middle to scale it whole; while a scale is dragged only what it goes along shows, each cube going out or in with its scale from a ring where it was, and by how much (and the scale it gives) beside it; always along the selection's own axes (a scale can only stretch along those), so on a turned entity its handles are turned with it. Each drag is one command. Snapping rounds what a drag gives, not the drag: moving in the world's axes puts the position on the grid, turning about a ring puts the selection's own angle about that axis on a whole step (40 degrees turned on at 15 goes to 45 or 60), scaling puts each scale on a whole step; rolling the sphere turns in whole steps. The toolbar's icons are the user's own set (`icons/`; the pivot and centre icons were added in its style), drawn as vectors (`icons.h`): sharp at any scale, no textures.
- **Surface snapping** (the toolbar's ground button after the scale snap, kept between runs): a move across the ground (along X or Z, in the ground's plane, or the free middle sphere) keeps what's moved on it, as End would put it there (`GroundSnap`, gizmo.h); moves up or down are left as dragged, so it can still be lifted.
- **Camera bookmarks:** Ctrl+1 to 9 keeps where the camera is, 1 to 9 goes back there (not while typing); per world, in `user:/editor/bookmarks.settings`. In the console: `editor.bookmark N`, `editor.bookmark N set`.
- **Colliders** (the toolbar's two cube buttons, after surface snapping; kept between runs): all of physics' colliders within 60 m of the camera (`physics.show_colliders`), or only the selection's (`physics.show_selected_colliders`: each chosen entity's collider, or its nearest parent's, wherever it is, and the ground's near the camera when the terrain is chosen), as lines along their triangles, solid where in view and faint where hidden, colored by kind (static blue, ground green, moving orange or grey asleep, characters yellow).
- **Cell grid** (View > Cell Grid, `--cell-grid`, kept between runs): the 128 m cells' borders on the ground (the terrain's, else the world's zero) round the camera, each cell's coordinates in its middle.
- **Hierarchy** (`outline.h`, `hierarchy_panel.h`): first, the world's **Terrain** when it has one (`editor.select Terrain`, `--select Terrain`): chosen, it's outlined round its edges and skyline and the properties show what it is (size, origin, height bounds); F (or a double-click on its row, or `editor.frame` in the console) frames all of it, looked down on from about 35 degrees; it has no handles and isn't moved, turned, scaled, duplicated or deleted. A click on the ground in the Playground still chooses nothing. Then what's placed, by name, each with what's attached to it (`flecs::Parent` or `ChildOf`); the editor's own camera is left out. Cells don't show: they're storage (docs/design.md, World model: nobody files things into cells by hand). Names come from the entity's flecs name, or its name in its cell's file (`world::CellMember`); what it is and its cell show on a pause over it. Arrow keys move and open; a selection made elsewhere opens the tree down to it. It reads the world four times a second, not every frame.
- **Cell map** (`cell_map_panel.h`): the world from above: the terrain's extent, the 128 m grid, the loaded cells, the camera and where it looks, the selection. The wheel zooms, the right or middle button drags; it follows the camera until dragged (Center). A click selects a cell, a double-click sends the camera there.
- **Properties** (`properties_panel.h`): a category per component, a row per field, made from flecs reflection: any component registered with `.member<>()` shows without code here. Field names read as words with their unit (`vertical_fov_rad`: "Vertical Fov (rad)"); small vectors edit on one row, tinted by axis, each number with as many decimals as its field has room for (the whole value on a pause over it); a reset arrow shows on a field you've changed and puts back what it was (the command stack keeps each component's value from before its first edit). A `Transform`'s rotation shows as angles. What the engine computes (world transforms, a cell's hold, render slots), what names assets, and anything that isn't reflected plain data stays hidden behind a link at the bottom, and is read only.
- **Menus:** File, Edit (undo, redo, framing), View (Frame Stats), Rendering Settings (temporal anti-aliasing), Preferences (the interface scale), Window (reset the layout). The scene's views (lit, base color and the rest) are the Playground toolbar's.
- **Console** (`): over the bottom of the Playground, as in a game (app/README.md, console/README.md).
- **Frame stats** (F1, or View > Frame Stats) start hidden in the editor; they show over the Playground's top-left, see-through, taking no clicks, drawn as a part of the Playground: a drawer or a menu over it covers them. `--stats` picks what they show and starts them shown (app/README.md).
- **Saving** (`cell_saver.h`, `cell_source.h`): File > Save, Ctrl+S or `editor.save` in the console. (`editor.move NAME X Y Z` moves something by name, as an undoable command, for agents and scripts.) Each loaded cell's members are compared with what the cell was cooked from; what moved, turned or scaled is written into its `.cell` source, relative to its parent there, only the values that changed (a position to 0.1 mm, angles to 0.001 degrees, as `rotation_deg` or the quaternion the entry already uses), so comments, key order and each entry's layout stay. A file edited since its cook is refused, not written into the wrong entries. The cooker then runs in the background (`--no-shaders`) and hot reload spawns the cells' members again; the selection and the undo stack follow them by their place in the source (`MemberTracker`). Fog volumes' values are saved the same way, key by key in their `fog_volume` object. A root moved into another cell's square goes into that cell's file in the same world (the one open, among the worlds listing its file), with everything that names it as a parent: renamed with a suffix if its name is taken there (its children follow), its mesh's path made from the new file's folder. A cell with no file gets `cell_X_Z.cell` next to the file it left, and the world's `cells` list gets it; the files things move into are written before the files they leave. The status bar says when there are unsaved edits and while it cooks; closing the window, File > Quit or opening another world with unsaved edits asks first (Save, Don't Save, Cancel).
- **Folders** (`hierarchy_panel.h`): the designer's own groups in the hierarchy, folders first, nested by `/`. A click on a folder chooses everything in it; a double-click (or F2) renames it in place; rows dragged onto a folder go into it, onto the space below the rows out of every folder; a folder dragged onto another goes inside it. The right button: Move to New Folder, Move Out of Folders, and on a folder Rename and Remove Folder (what's in it moves up a level, nothing is deleted). Each is a command. An entity's folder is its `EditorFolder`, read from its entry's `"folder"` in its cell's file when the cell spawns and written back there at a save; the cooker takes the key and leaves it out: folders are only for organising. In the console: `editor.folder PATH [NAME ...]`.
- **Choosing several** (`selection.h`): a click in the Playground or on a hierarchy row chooses only that; Ctrl toggles one in or out, Shift adds one (in the hierarchy, the rows from the one chosen last). The one chosen last is the primary: the properties show it, the handles sit on it (or at the centre of them all, with Center). A drag moves, turns or scales them all about the same point, one undo for them all (the commands' groups); what's attached to another chosen one follows it rather than moving twice. Delete, Ctrl+D and End do each of them, one command for them all; the copies are what's chosen after a duplicate. In the console: `editor.select NAME ...`, and `editor.delete`, `editor.duplicate`, `editor.ground` with no name act on the selection.
- **Placing, duplicating, deleting** (`entity_record.h`): a model (.gltf, .glb, .fbx) dragged from Resources over the Playground shows itself under the mouse where it would go (the model itself, not yet a command; out of the Playground or the drag let go elsewhere, it goes; `--preview PATH` holds one over the middle for a picture), and let go it's placed where the ray under the mouse meets the nearer of the terrain (`world::TerrainHeights`) and something solid (physics), else the level of the world's zero; Ctrl+D (Edit > Duplicate) copies the selection where it stands; Delete (Edit > Delete) takes it away, with everything attached to it (undo brings them all back, attached as they were). What's placed or duplicated is named apart from everything in the world at once: a trailing number counts up, keeping its width (`boulder_1` to `boulder_5` if 2 to 4 are taken, `rock_01` to `rock_02`). Each is a command: undo and redo make and take away entities again (`EntityMaker`, the editor), and every command naming one follows the new one. What the editor makes carries its own `.cell` entry (`EditorEntry`: its keys, its name, the folder its paths are from) until a save writes it into the file of the cell it stands in, named apart from what's there, its mesh's path from that file's folder; once its cell comes back cooked, the member spawned from that entry takes its place. A deleted member comes out of its file at the save; what the file hung on it becomes a root where it stands. End (Edit > Drop to Ground) drops the selection onto the ground below it, as Unreal's: its lowest point (the box round its mesh as it's turned and scaled) on the nearer of the terrain and something solid, not itself or what hangs on it; one buried under the terrain comes up onto it; a command like any move. In the console: `editor.ground NAME`, `editor.place PATH X Y Z`, `editor.drop PATH` (at the middle of the Playground, as a mouse drop there), `editor.duplicate NAME`, `editor.delete NAME`, `editor.undo`, `editor.redo`.
- **Commands** (`commands.h`): every edit is a `component.set` command with undo and redo; one drag of a field is one command. The command API (a text form, Lua, the agents' socket) grows from here.
- **Output**: on a dark ground, as the engine talking: what it logs (every channel, warnings and errors in their colours) and what the console runs and says, a filter over the lines, Clear. It follows new lines while it's scrolled to the end.
- **Resources** (`asset_browser.h`), in its drawer: the source tree's `content/` (through `src:/`): back, forward and refresh, the path a step per folder, favourite folders, view options (one or two columns, card size); the folder tree beside the folder's contents as a list or a grid of cards; a search over every file and folder; Ctrl and Shift select several. It comes back as it was left, from `user:/editor/resources.settings`: the view, the layout, the card size, the favourites, the folder shown, the folders open in the tree, what was selected there, the search and the folder tree's width. A world opens with a double-click (or its Open button in the properties), by the asset ID its meta's GUID gives. Files can be dragged out (`AssetBrowser::kSourceFilePayload`) onto the Playground to place them. A glTF's `.bin` (its buffers) doesn't show: the `.gltf` is the model. Files and folders are managed there (`file_ops.h`): Rename (F2, or the right button), Delete (Del: it says which files still name what goes, then sends it to the recycle bin with its meta), New Folder, and a drop onto a folder (in the tree or among the contents) moves what's dragged, or the whole selection if it's in it. A file or folder moved or renamed takes its meta along, so it keeps its asset ID, and every text source naming it by a relative path (`.cell`, `.world`, `.material`, `.terrain`, `.terrainlayers`, `.environment`, `.gltf`) is rewritten to follow it, as are a moved text source's own references; names are made path-safe (lowercase, `_` for spaces). In the console: `editor.move_file FROM TO`, `editor.new_folder PATH`, `editor.delete_file PATH...`. Models, textures and materials show a picture of themselves on their cards and rows, on a square a shade lighter than the panel (`thumbnails.h`): a model as its mesh, a texture as its image fitted to the square (data and normal maps as their texels are), a text material as a lit sphere in its colour, textures, roughness and metalness. The cooker draws each as it cooks the source (a model from its coarsest LOD sharp at 128 pixels, from the front, a little above and to the left, its base colours and textures lit by one light, alpha-tested leaves cut out) and keeps it as a small BC7 texture beside it (`asset::thumbnail_asset_id`, tools/cooker/thumbnail_cooker.h), so it's made once per cook and is never stale; the editor only reads those (16 KB each, a few a frame) into shared pages of 8 x 8 pictures, at most 4 (256 on screen at once, 16 MB, whatever the project's size): once they're full, the picture drawn longest ago gives its place to the next. Refresh, and a cook from the editor, read them again.

For pictures from the command line: `--select NAME` selects an entity, `--select-file PATH` a source file (and opens its folder in Resources), `--pick X,Y` clicks that pixel of the Playground's scene, `--tool NAME` and `--handles pivot|center` set the tool and where its handles sit, `--drag H,AMOUNT` shows a drag under way on a handle without changing anything, `--drawer resources|output` opens a drawer, `--show PANEL` brings a panel's tab to the front. A run that takes a picture saves neither the layout nor the settings.
