# render/scene

The scene on the GPU (docs/design.md, Rendering): GPU copies of the assets the registry has loaded, and the passes that draw what the world extracts each frame: deferred: a G-buffer, a full-screen lighting pass (the sun, a hemisphere ambient, the sky) and a tonemap, with the sun's shadows from cascaded shadow maps resolved into a screen-space mask.

## Contents

| File | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| `gpu_assets.h` | `GpuAssets`: meshes as vertex and index buffers, textures with their bindless index, materials in one table (`GpuMaterial`), mirrored from the registry's events |
| `scene_renderer.h` | `SceneRenderer`: the passes drawing a `world::RenderFrame`, `SceneLighting` (with the sun's `SceneShadows`, per frame) and `scene_lighting()` (from an environment asset), `SceneView` (debug views); `fit_shadow_cascades`, `camera_view_proj` (infinite reverse-Z, looking down -Z), `invert_4x4` |

## The G-buffer (v1)

| Target | Format | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | RGBA8 sRGB | base color; alpha: lighting group / 255 (0 until materials carry one) |
| 1 | RG16 unorm | world normal, octahedral, remapped to 0..1 |
| 2 | RGBA8 unorm | roughness, metalness, occlusion, translucency (0 until materials carry one) |
| HDR | RGBA16 float | emissive, written by the G-buffer pass; the lighting is added onto it |
| motion | RG16 float | uv now minus uv last frame, per pixel (0 where nothing was drawn: the sky moves with the camera alone) |
| depth | D32 float | reverse Z |

24 bytes a pixel plus depth. Motion comes from each instance's pose now and a frame ago (`world::RenderInstance`, six rows per instance in the frame's buffer) through this frame's camera and last frame's (`world::RenderView`: what was drawn, at this frame's render origin); after a camera cut, and on the first frame, the camera adds none. A surface that was behind last frame's camera has a motion off the screen, so its history is rejected. Bounded to 2 either way. Temporal anti-aliasing reads it. `shaders/scene/scene_common.slang` packs and unpacks it.

With TAA on, each frame is drawn with a sub-pixel offset (Halton 2, 3, 16 samples), and motion is measured without it. Depth, the resolved image and its object motion (TAA's own output: the motion target less the camera's part, from the nearest surface) are kept for the next frame: two sets of textures the renderer owns, swapped each frame, imported into the graph, made again when the size changes.

## The frame

1. `sun shadows`: four cascades in a 2 x 2 depth atlas (`SceneShadows::resolution` each, 2048 by default), every instance drawn into each, without culling. `fit_shadow_cascades` splits the view up to `distance_m` (practical split, `split_lambda`), bounds each slice with a sphere whose size doesn't change as the camera turns, and snaps its center to the texel grid so edges don't swim as the camera moves. Depth is reverse Z toward the sun, and reaches `caster_reach_m` past the sphere for casters outside the view.
2. `gbuffer`: every instance whose mesh is on the GPU, a draw per mesh part. Parts are grouped by kind each frame (one material lookup each): opaque, opaque double-sided, masked, masked double-sided, drawn in that order so masked pixels behind opaque surfaces fail early depth. Opaque parts use a shader with no `discard` (depth is tested before it runs), masked ones the alpha test; double-sided ones turn culling off (dynamic state, one shader for both). The shadow casters use the same groups, with culling off for every kind (thin geometry casts from both sides).
3. `shadow mask`: per pixel, the cascade by distance along the view, with a dithered band handing pixels to the next one (no visible seam; the last cascade fades to lit), the receiver pushed along its normal and toward the sun by about a texel (no acne), then a contact-hardening filter: a blocker search sets the penumbra from how far the occluder is (`penumbra_per_m`, up to `max_penumbra_texels`), 16 Poisson taps rotated per pixel by interleaved gradient noise (another value than the cascade dither's). RG8: sunlight seen, and the cascade (for its debug view). The light pass multiplies the sun by it, toward `SceneLighting::shadow_color` rather than black.
4. `deferred light`: one full-screen pass added onto the HDR target. The sun, through the shadow mask: GGX specular with height-correlated Smith visibility and Schlick Fresnel, Lambert diffuse; translucent surfaces get wrapped diffuse and light seen through them with the sun behind. The ambient: a sky/ground hemisphere for diffuse, and in the reflected direction through a fitted environment BRDF for specular (the placeholder for image-based lighting). Where nothing was drawn, the sky: a gradient to the horizon and the sun's disk.
5. `fog inject`, `fog integrate` (compute; only with `SceneFog::volume_distance_m`): the froxel grid, a column per 8 pixels and 64 slices spaced exponentially out to that distance, or the shadow distance if that's nearer (past the shadows it would add nothing to the closed form). Inject works out each froxel's light per unit of extinction, with the sun's shadows from the atlas (4 samples through the fog in the froxel's depth, fading to lit over the last cascade's band as the surfaces do), and its mean extinction, exact from the medium's closed form. Over frames, each froxel's samples move around inside it (Halton through the column and its depth, a different offset per froxel by gradient noise), and its light keeps 90% of last frame's at the same place in the world, read from two kept 3D textures. Light is stored weighted by extinction, so filtering across a froxel with no fog doesn't dim it (`shaders/common/fog.slang`). Its extinction takes no history, being exact. A cut, a frame not drawn, a new reach or a new size start again from this frame's light, in the froxel's middle. A sudden change of lighting fades in over about 20 frames. The kept textures go when the grid is off. Fog volumes (`world::RenderFogVolume`, in the frame: the nearest 64 in view and in the grid's reach, with a mask per slice of those whose bounding sphere reaches it) add their extinction and tint to the froxels they cover, tested at 3 points through where a froxel's middle ray is inside each one, so extinction stays steady and however thin a volume is, a far slice as thick as it can't lose it as the camera moves; they fade out over the last tenth of the grid's reach. They're lit from their first meter, wherever the sun's haze starts. They take the environment's fog colours, and with no grid they don't exist. Integrate walks each column front to back, each slice in closed form, and stores the in-scatter and optical depth from the camera through that slice (depth interpolates correctly between slices; a channel's transmittance comes from it when it's read). The grid honours `start_m`, `sun_start_m` and `end_m`.
6. `fog`: the lit image into a new one: each pixel dimmed by the fog between it and the camera and the fog's own light added (`SceneLighting::fog`, `shaders/common/fog.slang`: constant plus height fog, coloured extinction, a two-colour sun lobe and ambient). From the grid as far as it reaches, read at the pixel's depth; past it, or without one, closed form along the ray. Where the pixel's surface is inside a slice with fog volumes, the share of that slice it takes is what's really in front of the surface, tested along the pixel's own ray: by depth alone, the error sweeps across a volume resting on the ground as the slices slide over it with the camera, which looks like wind. The grid's boxes still move through a still volume, so a little of that stays at a distance. If the fog's shaders are broken, no fog (or no grid), not a cleared frame. The sky is fogged as far as `end_m`, so the horizon takes the land's haze. The sun and sky light the fog as they do where the light reaching the camera is mostly scattered in (the transmittance-weighted mean distance), through the column above it. No pass without fog, or in a debug view: the tonemap reads the lit image.
7. `taa` (only with TAA on, and not in a debug view): each pixel blends into its own history, read where its point was (`shaders/scene/taa.slang`), in a tonemapped space so a bright pixel neither dominates its neighbours nor flickers. The history is read with Catmull-Rom, which stays sharp where bilinear blurs a little more each frame, and keeps 92% unless something argues against it (see Temporal anti-aliasing below). The shadow mask's noise moves with the sample, so its dither averages out too.
8. `tonemap`: exposure (`SceneLighting::exposure_ev`) and a filmic curve (the ACES fit) into the target, or a G-buffer channel, the shadow mask or the cascades (`SceneView`, F2 in deengine).

## Temporal anti-aliasing

`add_passes(..., taa)` turns it on (`taa on` in `config/engine.settings`, F3 in dev builds). What keeps history from ghosting, in layers; each covers what the others can't:

- **Motion from the nearest surface** in the 3 x 3 around the pixel, so an object's edges travel with it. The sky has no motion written: it takes the camera's.
- **Colour clip:** history is clipped toward the neighbours' mean, in YCoCg, to the box of the colours around the pixel now. A colour that left can't stay. Most ghosts end here, but not next to an edge, where the old colour is still among the neighbours.
- **Object motion coherence:** history fades by how far the object motion at its old place, as kept last frame, differs from the object motion here (pixels squared, forgiving about a quarter of the speed): it was about something else. Without the camera's part, so a camera changing speed costs a static world nothing; in proportion to speed, so frame pacing costs what the camera follows nothing; from the nearest surface on both sides, so a silhouette's ring matches itself.
- **Depth:** where only the camera moved the point (object motion under 0.05 pixels), its depth is compared with the near and far depths around its old place (last frame's, 3 x 3, the sky as farther than anything, 1% slack). Outside them, a surface there has moved off or come in front, and history goes. Coming in front needs more than a sliver: most of the 3 x 3 at this depth, so a thin thing the jitter hits now and then keeps its history.
- **No history at all:** off the screen or behind last frame's camera, on the first frame, after a camera cut, after a frame not drawn with TAA (`RenderFrame::number` has a gap), or at a new size. A pixel softens as it loses history, to a soft blur of this frame without any, rather than raw aliasing.

No TAA is free of ghosts for what doesn't write depth or motion (particles, transparents): those will need a reactive mask when they arrive. Thin geometry fades rather than flashes: when the jitter misses it, the colour box has no room for it. Very near the point something flies straight at the camera from, its depth rule can still drop history.

## Rules and invariants

- **The registry owns what's loaded; this mirrors the GPU side.** `GpuAssets` listens to the registry: an asset uploads when it's Ready, again when it's Reloaded, and goes when it's Unloaded. Its maps are keyed by the registry's handles. It must exist before the assets it should mirror load (there's no catch-up), and it stops listening when it goes (`AssetRegistry::remove_listener`).
- **Nothing is drawn before its data is on the GPU.** A mesh becomes drawable in `update()` once the uploader has recorded its buffers (`Uploader::ready(BufferHandle)`); until then `mesh()` is null and the instance is skipped. A material whose textures are still uploading points at default textures (white, a flat normal) and is written again when they're in, so shaders never branch on a missing texture.
- **Material 0 is the default**; a part whose material is missing or failed uses it. Table slots are reused when materials unload; the table holds `GpuAssetsDesc::max_materials` (4096).
- **Reloads replace GPU copies.** A reloaded texture gets a new GPU texture (and bindless index); every material is rewritten, and the old texture stays until the table no longer points at it. A reloaded mesh gets new buffers and is drawable again once they're uploaded. The old resources go when the frames using them are done (the device's deferred destruction).
- **The renderer owns no entity list.** `SceneRenderer::add_passes` reads `world::RenderFrame`: the camera and every instance, relative to the render origin. Per-frame data (view, lighting, instance transforms) goes through a mapped buffer per frame in flight, grown when needed: no allocation per frame once settled.
- **Cameras look down their -Z**; projection is infinite reverse Z (depth = near / distance, cleared to 0, tested Greater).
- Without a camera, while a scene shader is missing (a failed first build), or when the frame's constants can't be written (no per-frame buffer, or a camera transform that can't be inverted), the passes are replaced by one that clears the target.
