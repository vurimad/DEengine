# render/scene

The scene on the GPU (docs/design.md, Rendering): GPU copies of the assets the registry has loaded, and the passes that draw what the world extracts each frame: deferred: a G-buffer, a full-screen lighting pass (the sun, a hemisphere ambient, the sky) and a tonemap, with the sun's shadows from cascaded shadow maps resolved into a screen-space mask.

## The G-buffer (v1)

| Target | Format | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | RGBA8 sRGB | base color; alpha: lighting group / 255 (0 until materials carry one) |
| 1 | RG16 unorm | world normal, octahedral, remapped to 0..1 |
| 2 | RGBA8 unorm | roughness, metalness, occlusion, translucency (0 until materials carry one) |
| HDR | RGBA16 float | emissive, written by the G-buffer pass; the lighting is added onto it |
| motion | RG16 float | uv now minus uv last frame, per pixel (0 where nothing was drawn: the sky moves with the camera alone) |
| depth | D32 float | reverse Z |

24 bytes a pixel plus depth. Motion comes from each instance's pose now and a frame ago (`world::RenderInstance`, a `GpuInstance` in the instance buffer) through this frame's camera and last frame's (`world::RenderView`: what was drawn, at this frame's render origin); after a camera cut, and on the first frame, the camera adds none. A surface that was behind last frame's camera has a motion off the screen, so its history is rejected. Bounded to 2 either way. Temporal anti-aliasing reads it. `shaders/scene/scene_common.slang` packs and unpacks it.

With TAA on, each frame is drawn with a sub-pixel offset (Halton 2, 3, 16 samples), and motion is measured without it. Depth, the resolved image and its object motion (TAA's own output: the motion target less the camera's part, from the nearest surface) are kept for the next frame: two sets of textures the renderer owns, swapped each frame, imported into the graph, made again when the size changes.

## The frame

1. `sun shadows`: four cascades in a 2 x 2 depth atlas (`SceneShadows::resolution` each, 2048 by default), the instances the cull found in each. `fit_shadow_cascades` splits the view up to `distance_m` (practical split, `split_lambda`), bounds each slice with a sphere whose size doesn't change as the camera turns, and snaps its center to the texel grid so edges don't swim as the camera moves. Depth is reverse Z toward the sun, and reaches `caster_reach_m` past the sphere for casters outside the view.
2. `gbuffer`: every instance whose mesh is on the GPU, a draw per mesh part, except parts that blend (step 7). Parts are grouped by kind each frame (one material lookup each): opaque, opaque double-sided, masked, masked double-sided, drawn in that order so masked pixels behind opaque surfaces fail early depth. Opaque parts use a shader with no `discard` (depth is tested before it runs), masked ones the alpha test; double-sided ones turn culling off (dynamic state, one shader for both). The shadow casters use the same groups, with culling off for every kind (thin geometry casts from both sides).
3. `shadow mask`: per pixel, the cascade by distance along the view, with a dithered band handing pixels to the next one (no visible seam; the last cascade fades to lit), the receiver pushed along its normal and toward the sun by about a texel (no acne), then a contact-hardening filter: a blocker search sets the penumbra from how far the occluder is (`penumbra_per_m`, up to `max_penumbra_texels`), 16 Poisson taps rotated per pixel by interleaved gradient noise (another value than the cascade dither's). RG8: sunlight seen, and the cascade (for its debug view). The light pass multiplies the sun by it, toward `SceneLighting::shadow_color` rather than black.
4. `deferred light`: one full-screen pass added onto the HDR target. The sun, through the shadow mask: GGX specular with height-correlated Smith visibility and Schlick Fresnel, Lambert diffuse; translucent surfaces get wrapped diffuse and light seen through them with the sun behind. The ambient: a sky/ground hemisphere for diffuse, and in the reflected direction through a fitted environment BRDF for specular (the placeholder for image-based lighting). Where nothing was drawn, the sky: a gradient to the horizon and the sun's disk.
4a. `light cull` (compute; only with a local light reaching the view): the view cut into 32 x 18 x 32 clusters (a grid across the picture, slices that thicken with distance from 0.5 m to 512 m), a thread a cluster testing its box against each of the frame's lights (`LocalLights::update` keeps those whose sphere touches the view, 256 at most, the nearest for their size) and writing a bit per light. It runs before the deferred light, which adds the lights of each pixel's cluster as it does the sun (`shaders/scene/local_lights.slang`: inverse square to nothing at the light's radius, a spot's cone); the transparent pass and the water (mirrored only) read the same words. `SceneFrame` carries where the lights and the words are; with no lights its `light_grid.w` is 0 and nothing reads them. No shadows yet.
4b. `trace bottom levels`, `trace instances`, `trace top level` (only where the renderer traces: `SceneRendererDesc::ray_tracing` on a GPU with ray queries; right after the instance update, before anything is drawn). `TraceScene` keeps a bottom level for each mesh an instance uses: `write_instance_updates` says which (`want`), `update` queues a frame's share of builds (16) from the geometry pool, and a mesh is in the table rays read (`GpuMeshTrace` by mesh slot) from the frame after its build. The LOD is `trace_lod`'s: the coarsest within 2 cm of the finest that has every part and kept its seams. A bottom level goes when no instance uses its mesh, or the mesh went, moved in the pool or was cooked again (looked for when the registry or the pool says so, and every 120 frames). `trace instances` (compute, `shaders/scene/trace_instances.slang`) writes the top level's instances from the instance table, a thread a slot: where it stands relative to the render origin, its mesh's bottom level, or no instance; `trace top level` builds it whole from them. `SceneFrame::trace` points at the frame's `GpuTraceScene` (the top level's address and the tables a hit is read through), and shaders trace with `shaders/scene/trace.slang`. `SceneView::Traced` (`trace view`, after the deferred light, in its place) shows what a ray a pixel meets, lit by the sun through a shadow ray and by the sky; where nothing is traced, it's the lit view without what's drawn after the light.
4b'. `ao depth`, `gtao`, `ao denoise` x2 (compute; in the lit view and the views of the ambient, with `set_ambient_occlusion` on): after the G-buffer, before the light. `Gtao::add_passes` makes the depth pyramid (R32F, 5 mips), the occlusion (R8) and its two denoised copies, all transient; the deferred light reads the last for its ambient only (the darker of it and the material's occlusion, through the multi-bounce fit for diffuse and a specular occlusion for the reflection), and the tonemap shows it in `SceneView::AmbientOcclusion`.
4c. `light box bake` (compute; only where rays are traced, while a box has voxels left to bake): `LightBoxes::add_passes`, after the top level is built and before the deferred light; each frame some voxels add their share of paths, about a million in all, and rewrite their maps from the sums.
4d. `rc glow` (before the deferred light: what surfaces emit, at the GI picture's size), then `rc pyramid`, `radiance cascade` x as many as reach across the GI picture less one, `rc gather`, `rc apply` (with `set_radiance_cascades` on, in the lit and indirect light views): after the deferred light, whose picture the rays meet; the topmost cascade first, each merging the one above; `rc gather` keeps the light's moments at each of the GI picture's pixels (and, for the next frame's `rc pyramid`, what each surface sends on of that light: more than one bounce), and `rc apply`, the only one at the screen's size after the glow, adds the light along each pixel's normal and what the pixel mirrors (the specular ambient, from the same moments) onto the HDR target (the light alone, in place of it, in the indirect light view); the deferred light adds no ambient of its own while the cascades are on, and what the screen can't answer, fog, lit particles, water and see-through things take the sky's ambient by how open to the sky their place is (`shaders/scene/sky.slang`'s `open_sky`: the environment's `under_roof`, and a fog volume's for its own place).
5. `fog inject`, `fog integrate` (compute; only with `SceneFog::volume_distance_m`): the froxel grid, a column per 8 pixels and 64 slices spaced exponentially out to that distance, or the shadow distance if that's nearer (past the shadows it would add nothing to the closed form). Inject works out each froxel's light per unit of extinction, with the sun's shadows from the atlas (4 samples through the fog in the froxel's depth, fading to lit over the last cascade's band as the surfaces do), and its mean extinction, exact from the medium's closed form. Over frames, each froxel's samples move around inside it (Halton through the column and its depth, a different offset per froxel by gradient noise), and its light keeps 90% of last frame's at the same place in the world, read from two kept 3D textures. Light is stored weighted by extinction, so filtering across a froxel with no fog doesn't dim it (`shaders/common/fog.slang`). Its extinction takes no history, being exact. A cut, a frame not drawn, a new reach or a new size start again from this frame's light, in the froxel's middle. A sudden change of lighting fades in over about 20 frames. The kept textures go when the grid is off, or when fog volumes alone were keeping it on and it's been out of view for 240 frames. Fog volumes (`world::RenderFogVolume`, in the frame: the nearest 64 in view and in the grid's reach, with a mask per slice of those whose bounding sphere reaches it) add their extinction and tint to the froxels they cover, tested at 3 points through where a froxel's middle ray is inside each one, so extinction stays steady and however thin a volume is along the ray, a far slice as thick as it can't lose it as the camera moves (across the view, one thinner than a column can still fall between columns); they fade out over the last tenth of the grid's reach. They're lit from their first meter, wherever the sun's haze starts, by samples of where they are, not the rest of the slice. Noise can eat into them (`GpuAssets::fog_noise_index()`: a tiling 32-cubed texture of our own, `fog_noise.h`, made at startup): so thin edges go first, to wisps, and a heart can lose as much as `noise_amount`: `density_per_m` is the most a volume has. It belongs to the world, not the volume: it has three sizes, and drifts by the world's time. Each point reads the mip its footprint can hold (its share of the slice's depth, or the column's width), so a far slice sees big soft shapes, not noise it would land on differently as the camera moves. A height fade thins a volume toward its top, along the world's up. The fog pass places volumes by their shape alone. They take the environment's fog colours, and with no grid they don't exist. Integrate walks each column front to back, each slice in closed form, and stores the in-scatter and optical depth from the camera through that slice (depth interpolates correctly between slices; a channel's transmittance comes from it when it's read). The grid honours `start_m`, `sun_start_m` and `end_m`.
6. `fog`: the lit image into a new one: each pixel dimmed by the fog between it and the camera and the fog's own light added (`SceneLighting::fog`, `shaders/common/fog.slang`: constant plus height fog, coloured extinction, a two-colour sun lobe and ambient). From the grid as far as it reaches, read at the pixel's depth; past it, or without one, closed form along the ray. Where the pixel's surface is inside a slice with fog volumes, the share of that slice it takes is what's really in front of the surface, tested along the pixel's own ray: by depth alone, the error sweeps across a volume resting on the ground as the slices slide over it with the camera, which looks like wind. The grid's boxes still move through a still volume, so a little of that stays at a distance. If the fog's shaders are broken, no fog (or no grid), not a cleared frame. The sky is fogged as far as `end_m`, so the horizon takes the land's haze. The sun and sky light the fog as they do where the light reaching the camera is mostly scattered in (the transmittance-weighted mean distance), through the column above it. No pass without fog, or in a debug view: the tonemap reads the lit image.
7. `transparent measure`, `transparent sort`, `transparents` (only in the lit view, and only while something among the instances blends): what blends (`asset::AlphaMode::Blend`, `GpuMaterialFlags::Blend`) over the fogged image, the farthest first. The cull keeps those parts out of every other list and in one of their own, for the camera alone, a draw each (never merged, one LOD, no shadow). `transparent measure` (compute) gives each draw the distance of its instance's bounding sphere's middle along the view; `transparent sort` counts, for each, how many are farther, which is its place (every draw against every other: at most 4,096 a view, far fewer in practice; two at one distance keep the cull's order, so nothing flickers); `transparents` draws them in that order with one counted indirect call. Each is lit as it's drawn, as the light pass lights what's solid (the sun through one filtered sample of its cascade, the ambient), from both sides, and mixed premultiplied: its own colour by its opacity, what it reflects and emits in full (below 5% opacity what it reflects fades out too: a glow has no surface). It applies the fog in front of itself (the grid, then the closed form; without the fog volumes' share of a slice). It tests against the scene's depth and writes none, and no motion: its second target, `transparent coverage`, adds up how much of each pixel it covers, for TAA. One order per object: two that pass through each other are wrong on one side.
7b. `particles clear`, `particles step`, `particles` (only in the lit view, and only with a registry: `SceneRendererDesc::assets`): the frame's effects (`world::RenderFrame::effects`), by `ParticleSystem`.
   - **The pool.** 1,048,576 particles of 32 bytes (place, age, velocity, seed) in blocks of 64. An emitter instance takes a run of blocks for its `capacity` when the camera comes within its effect's `draw_distance_m` (first fit, on the CPU: `ParticleBlocks`) and gives it back past that; a run just taken is filled with "no particle" by `particles clear`. With no run free it waits (`ParticleStats::emitters_starved`).
   - **Steps.** Each effect has its own clock (`world::EffectClock`): its age at the frame's time, in whole steps of 1/60 s, is how many steps its emitters should have taken, and what's left over is how far the draw carries its particles on (`since_step_s`, per emitter). `particles step` runs once for each step some emitter in view is short of: none on most frames above 60 fps. Each emitter keeps how many steps it has done and how many particles were born by then (`particles_born`: whole numbers of its own clock); for each step the CPU writes which slot of its ring the first newborn goes in and how many there are, and lists its blocks. The compute pass (`shaders/scene/particles_step.slang`, a group to a block) gives each newborn its seed, its place and its velocity from the birth modules, born part-way through the step by its place among them, and moves every other live particle by the step modules. Nothing in it depends on the frame rate or on another particle, so the same steps leave the same pool: tested at 30, 60 and 240 fps.
   - **Behind.** An emitter out of view (its `reach_m`, scaled and with wind and its own motion added, against the view with a margin) isn't stepped. Back in view, new, back in reach, or with its effect's clock set ahead, it's short of its effect's age: it takes the steps it's short of, up to 64 a frame (`ParticleSystemDesc::catch_up_steps`; the effect viewer's scene takes 1,024, so dragging its time never empties the picture), and isn't drawn until it has; short by more than its longest lifetime, its run is cleared and it starts that many steps back, which leaves exactly the particles stepping all the way would have. Ahead of its clock (the clock was set back), it starts from nothing the same way. So an effect's particles are what its age gives, however it got there. `prewarm_s` is steps on top of the age.
   - **The draw.** One instanced draw (`shaders/scene/particles.slang`): a quad to a particle, 64 to a listed block, the blocks of the farthest emitter first, onto the fogged image and the transparent pass's coverage (made here if nothing see-through drew), premultiplied, tested against the scene's depth. The vertex shader carries each particle on from the last step by its velocity, works out its size, colour, opacity and turn from the draw modules at its age, lights it if its look says (the sun through one cascade sample, the sky and ground), and fogs it; a slot with no particle becomes six vertices at one point. The fragment shader draws the shape (`glow`, `disc`, `puff`, `spark`) and fades it into a surface closer than `soft_m` behind it and into the camera.
   - **What a frame costs the CPU:** a visit to every effect in the world (its distance, its sphere against the view, its steps), and the frame's emitters, steps, modules and lists copied into one mapped buffer. Nothing per particle.
8. `taa` (only with TAA on, and not in a debug view): each pixel blends into its own history, read where its point was (`shaders/scene/taa.slang`), in a tonemapped space so a bright pixel neither dominates its neighbours nor flickers. The history is read with Catmull-Rom, which stays sharp where bilinear blurs a little more each frame, and keeps 92% unless something argues against it (see Temporal anti-aliasing below). The shadow mask's noise moves with the sample, so its dither averages out too.
9. `tonemap`: exposure (`SceneLighting::exposure_ev`) and a filmic curve (the ACES fit) into the target, or a G-buffer channel, the shadow mask or the cascades (`SceneView`, F2 in deengine).

The editor's (dev builds, `set_editor_overlay`): while it asks, `gbuffer` writes each pixel's instance slot plus one into an R32 target (its programs built again with `EDITOR_IDS`; the ground and grass write `kEditorTerrainId`, never picked), and a pick copies one texel of it to a readback buffer, read when that frame slot comes round again (`pick_answer`). With the terrain chosen (`EditorOverlay::terrain_selected`), `editor terrain selection`, a compute pass, makes the mask from the IDs instead: whatever isn't sky, so the outline follows the ground's edges and skyline. Otherwise `editor selection` draws the selection's meshes alone (LOD 0, unjittered) into a mask, covered and seen (nearer than the scene's depth, with slack for the jitter), and `editor outline`, after the tonemap, draws an orange band around it, fainter where it's hidden.

## Temporal anti-aliasing

`add_passes(..., taa)` turns it on (`taa on` in `config/engine.settings`, F3 in dev builds). What keeps history from ghosting, in layers; each covers what the others can't:

- **Motion from the nearest surface** in the 3 x 3 around the pixel, so an object's edges travel with it. The sky has no motion written: it takes the camera's.
- **Colour clip:** history is clipped toward the neighbours' mean, in YCoCg, to the box of the colours around the pixel now. A colour that left can't stay. Most ghosts end here, but not next to an edge, where the old colour is still among the neighbours.
- **Object motion coherence:** history fades by how far the object motion at its old place, as kept last frame, differs from the object motion here (pixels squared, forgiving about a quarter of the speed): it was about something else. Without the camera's part, so a camera changing speed costs a static world nothing; in proportion to speed, so frame pacing costs what the camera follows nothing; from the nearest surface on both sides, so a silhouette's ring matches itself.
- **Depth:** where only the camera moved the point (object motion under 0.05 pixels), its depth is compared with the near and far depths around its old place (last frame's, 3 x 3, the sky as farther than anything, 1% slack). Outside them, a surface there has moved off or come in front, and history goes. Coming in front needs more than a sliver: most of the 3 x 3 at this depth, so a thin thing the jitter hits now and then keeps its history.
- **No history at all:** off the screen or behind last frame's camera, on the first frame, after a camera cut, after a frame not drawn with TAA (`RenderFrame::number` has a gap), or at a new size. A pixel softens as it loses history, to a soft blur of this frame without any, rather than raw aliasing.
- **What's see-through** (the transparent pass) writes no depth and no motion, so none of the above knows it's there: history gives up half its weight where it covers the whole pixel, less where it covers less (`transparent coverage`). A fast see-through thing still ghosts a little: it says how much of the pixel changed, not where it came from.

Thin geometry fades rather than flashes: when the jitter misses it, the colour box has no room for it. Very near the point something flies straight at the camera from, its depth rule can still drop history.

## Terrain

A map is a pyramid of nodes (`asset/terrain_format.h`). What's loaded follows the camera from the first version, on rings of its own, not with the world's cells: terrain never holds up a cell.

- **Streaming.** Each frame `Terrain::update` asks which nodes are wanted (`terrain_wanted_nodes`: every root, and what's within its level's range and a margin, looking only around the camera). Blocks are read one at a time into a pool of fixed slots (`TerrainDesc::max_blocks`, 36 MiB at 1,024), coarsest and nearest first, at most 16 in flight, and uploaded. A node shows in the table of slots only once its upload is recorded and its parent shows, and goes only once its children have, so the table always holds whole chains from a root. What's let go waits for the frames in flight before its slot is reused. Over capacity, what's held and still wanted stays, the rest waits, and it warns once (`TerrainStats::over_capacity`). A node that can't be read fails alone: what's coarser stands in. Heights from another cook are refused whole.
- **Choosing.** `terrain select` walks a flat table of every patch of each level, and each decides for itself from the camera's distance, its height bounds and the table of slots: drawn if it's near enough for its level and its children aren't, or aren't there. So any state of loading is one whole surface, and the cost follows the map's patches (0.03 ms is the estimate at 60,000, unmeasured). It's the same choice for the camera and every cascade, so they agree on the ground, and each view then keeps what's in it, through the cull's views, so a frozen cull freezes which patches are kept. LOD still follows the live camera.
- **Drawing.** One indexed draw per view, instanced over its list: 32 x 32 squares, and for the camera a skirt on each side. Vertices read their height from the block by address, 16-bit, with whole steps per level. Positions are whole steps off the map's corner and then off the render origin, so they hold anywhere. The ground draws first in `gbuffer`, and hides the most. `F2` has a view tinting it by level.
- **Morph.** Within the last 30% of its level's range, a vertex turns into what the coarser level's surface is at that point, height only, so an edge between levels meets and a swap moves nothing. Skirts cover what morphing can't: a neighbour two levels away while it loads, and the edge of the map, where vertices also stop.

Not yet: materials, baked normals (derived from the heights in the vertex shader, so far land is smooth), far shadows (the ground casts into the cascades only, 140 m), patch back-face culling, collision, compression. Timings haven't been measured on a quiet GPU.

## Rules and invariants

- **The registry owns what's loaded; this mirrors the GPU side.** `GpuAssets` listens to the registry: an asset uploads when it's Ready, again when it's Reloaded, and goes when it's Unloaded. Its maps are keyed by the registry's handles. It must exist before the assets it should mirror load (there's no catch-up), and it stops listening when it goes (`AssetRegistry::remove_listener`).
- **Every mesh lives in the geometry pool:** one vertex and one index buffer (`GpuAssetsDesc::pool_vertices`, `pool_indices`), made once, so a draw needs only an offset and one indirect draw can reach any mesh. A place being filled is read by nothing (`rhi::BufferRange::Unread`), so streaming a mesh doesn't make the frame wait, though the buffer is in use. A mesh gets a range of each when it loads. If none fits, it's logged, counted in `meshes_dropped`, and not drawn; the pool doesn't grow, and the mesh is tried again as places come back. Its place is freed when it's unloaded or reloaded, once the frames that may still draw from it are done. Bounds and every LOD are kept (`GpuMesh`).
- **A draw finds what it needs through its first instance.** Shaders read a `GpuDraw` record (instance row, material slot, LOD fade) at `SV_VulkanInstanceID`, so nothing changes per draw on the CPU: the G-buffer makes one indirect call per pipeline state, of which what's fading has its own, and each shadow cascade two at most, whatever the draw count (`SceneRendererStats::indirect_calls`). A draw of N instances reads N consecutive records, so one draw per object and one draw for many both work with no shader change.
- **Instances stay on the GPU between frames.** A frame's `RenderInstance`s are what changed, each for its slot, and a compute pass scatters them into the instance buffers (`SceneRendererDesc::max_instances`, made once). Their positions are the world's, in whole steps of 1/16384 m, and shaders take the render origin off them (`instance_point`), so the origin moving sends nothing. A slot holds a stable slot in the mesh table (`GpuAssets::mesh_slot`), not a mesh's place, so a mesh that loads later or reloads needs nothing sent: its entry says it has no LODs until it can be drawn. What the draw lists can hold comes from how many instances there are of each mesh, not from walking them. If a frame's changes don't arrive (no camera, a shader missing, a frame not drawn), `instances_stale()` says so, and the next frame has to hold them all.
- **The GPU culls.** The CPU writes nothing per part. `scene.cull` tests every instance's bounding sphere against the camera and each shadow cascade, and uses the parts of the coarsest LOD that's good enough in that view, drawn with counted indirect calls. Records have room for all that could land in them, sized from `GpuMesh::kind_parts`, which costs memory: 12 bytes per part per view, and 24 more for the camera if the mesh has LODs, as fading draws two. A part that has no room in its list, for the one frame a material takes to be counted again, or past a device's call limit, is left out whole (`draws_lost`). `visible_draws`, `visible_shadow_draws` and the triangle counts show what it kept, read back a frame or two late.
- **A cascade draws only what can shadow what reads it.** `fit_shadow_cascades` gives each cascade the part of the view it's read for, with its blend band and what its filter reaches, swept toward the sun: at most 11 planes. Anything outside can't land a shadow on a pixel that reads this cascade, so no picture changes (`set_shadow_caster_cull(false)` is there to compare). `set_shadow_min_texels` can also keep casters narrower than that many of a cascade's texels out of it, which can change a picture, so it's off by default. A cache of far cascades couldn't use the sweep as it is, because what's cached has to hold for a camera that has since turned.
- **Draws merge.** Everything of one mesh part in one group (the camera's plain draws, what's fading, each cascade) is one instanced draw, so a draw's cost doesn't grow with the objects. `scene.cull` counts per part and notes each part the first time it's used; `scene.cull_assign` gives each of those a draw and a run of records; `scene.cull` writes the records; and the used keys go back to 0. So after the walk over instances, the cost follows what's in view, not what's loaded, and nothing's cleared whole: the buffers stay, at 48 bytes per part the table can hold (12 MB at the default 256k). A record's place in its run depends on the threads, but draw shaders find theirs by `SV_VulkanInstanceID`, so nothing else changes. `visible_draws` still counts objects' parts, and `merged_draws` the draws they came to. `set_merged_draws(false)` gives a draw per record, to compare, or for a GPU slow at very small instances.
- **Each view picks its own LOD,** the coarsest whose cooked error (of its shape, normals, uvs and colors, `mesh_cooker.cpp`) stays within a pixel on screen, or a texel in a shadow map, at the nearest depth of the instance's sphere. `visible_triangles` and `visible_shadow_triangles` show what it comes to, read back like the draws.
- **LODs fade into each other for the camera,** as a dither: each pixel goes to one LOD or the other, and TAA blends them. A LOD comes in where its error reaches the limit, so it's within the limit all the way, and takes over as its error falls by a share of it (`set_lod_fade`, 0.2, or sooner if the next LOD is near). The share comes from the error alone, so nothing's kept per instance and nothing is sent. What's fading draws twice, without early depth, from lists and pipelines of its own, and everything else pays nothing (`visible_fading_draws`). An object that stops mid-fade stays that way. Shadow cascades cut between LODs.
- **Draw order within a list isn't stable:** it follows the GPU's threads, so two coplanar surfaces with different materials can trade places from frame to frame. Decals shouldn't be built as coplanar meshes.
- **The cull can be frozen, to check it** (`set_cull_frozen`, F4 or `--freeze-cull` in deengine's dev builds; the stats panel and a `cull frozen` profiler plot say when it is). It can judge camera culling and LODs from any distance, and shadow casters only for small moves. Each view the cull reads, the camera's and every cascade's, with its LOD limits and smallest caster, stays as it was on the first frame after that. They're copied from a CPU copy each frame, each plane moved by what the render origin has moved since, so they stay in the same place in the world. Everything else stays live:
  - The G-buffer draws from the live camera what the frozen camera kept, at the LODs picked there. An object out of the frozen view is missing, the sky shows through, and a fade stays as far along as it was.
  - The shadow atlas and the mask use cascades fitted to the live camera (their matrices are never frozen), and draw into each tile what the same frozen cascade kept. A shadow from a caster that's there lands where it should, and a caster out of the frozen camera's view but in a frozen cascade casts a shadow with nothing there to cast it. After flying far away, though, shadows vanish whether the caster cull was right or wrong, because what the live cascades need isn't in those lists: a missing shadow says nothing about it then. By eye, the caster cull can only be judged for small moves, while the live cascades still cover the ground the frozen ones did. From farther, what can still be trusted is what it kept: `visible_shadow_draws` and `visible_shadow_triangles`, which come from the frozen views alone.
  - Instances that change, new ones included, are culled against the frozen views as usual. Fog volumes are picked on the CPU, and aren't frozen.
  - With TAA on, the camera's planes keep the jitter of the frame they froze on, under a pixel's worth. Unfrozen, the next frame is as usual.
- **Not culled yet:** instances hidden behind others (Hi-Z, which waits for real terrain).
- **Nothing is drawn before its data is on the GPU.** A mesh becomes drawable in `update()` once the uploader has recorded its uploads (by ticket: `Uploader::recorded`, since a shared buffer may always have another waiting); until then `mesh()` is null and the instance is skipped. A material whose textures are still uploading points at default textures (white, a flat normal) and is written again when they're in, so shaders never branch on a missing texture.
- **Material 0 is the default**; a part whose material is missing or failed uses it. Table slots are reused when materials unload; the table holds `GpuAssetsDesc::max_materials` (4096).
- **Reloads replace GPU copies.** A reloaded texture gets a new GPU texture (and bindless index); every material is rewritten, and the old texture stays until the table no longer points at it. A reloaded mesh gets a new place in the pool, keeps its slot in the mesh table, and is drawable again once it's uploaded. Old textures go when the frames using them are done (the device's deferred destruction), and old places the same way (`GpuAssetsState::free_retired_places`).
- **The renderer owns no entity list.** `SceneRenderer::add_passes` reads `world::RenderFrame`: the camera and what changed in the instances, relative to the render origin. Per-frame data (view, lighting, instance transforms) goes through a mapped buffer per frame in flight, grown when needed: no allocation per frame once settled.
- **Cameras look down their -Z**; projection is infinite reverse Z (depth = near / distance, cleared to 0, tested Greater).
- Without a camera, while a scene shader is missing (a failed first build), or when the frame's constants can't be written (no per-frame buffer, or a camera transform that can't be inverted), the passes are replaced by one that clears the target.

## Not yet

- For what blends: particles (the VFX system's, with a pool and a sort of their own), refraction, shadows cast by it, picking it in the editor by a click, weighted blended mixing for dense smoke, a low-resolution buffer.
- A static shadow cache (static casters drawn once, moving ones over them), local-light and cloud shadows, sun visibility for fog, temporal filtering of the mask; image-based lighting from probes, lighting groups with their key and fill lights, the diffuse/specular split (for subsurface), fog and haze, the tonemap curve as a table and grading LUTs, temporal anti-aliasing: the look study's list.
- Occlusion culling, impostors for the far field (GPU-driven v1).
- For the probes: reading them at half the picture's size (the deferred light's lookup is the largest cost), lighting spaces smaller than a coarse cascade's spacing from far away (they read dark), shadows for the local lights at what a ray meets, the probes' light on what's see-through and on particles (water has it: its own colour by the probes' light, its sun through the cascades, the sky it mirrors by the share of it the probes see), a clamp on fireflies.
- For rays: what's cut out or blends as anything but solid, the terrain, grass and water, a top level of only what's near the camera, compacted bottom levels, more than 64 parts a mesh, and its cost measured on a GPU that isn't shared (first numbers: docs/design.md, Global illumination).
- Streaming mips.

Up to `kMaxDebugLines` a frame, from a buffer per frame in flight.
